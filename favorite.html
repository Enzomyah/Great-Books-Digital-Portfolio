Section 03 - Favorite Masterpiece

A Work That
Stayed With Me.

The Odyssey - Homer

My Favorite Masterpiece

The Odyssey

By Homer  |  Ancient Greek Epic

Among the seven great works we studied in class, Homer's The Odyssey stood out to me the most. It is a powerful story about survival, quick thinking, and a ten-year struggle to return home. While many war stories focus only on fighting and winning battles, The Odyssey looks at what happens after the war ends: the hard journey of a man trying to cross dangerous seas and get back to the family he loves.

Odysseus is not a flawless superhero; he is a human leader whose greatest strength is his sharp mind, whose mistakes are very real, and whose story shows what it means to overcome hard times and protect home.

01

Why I Chose This Work

Reasons for Choosing It as My Favorite

I chose The Odyssey as my favorite masterpiece because it shows that true courage comes from intelligence, patience, and love for family, rather than just physical strength. Facing monsters, storms, and angry gods, Odysseus does not win by just overpowering his problems. Instead, he survives by thinking carefully, staying calm under pressure, and outsmarting dangerous enemies like the Cyclops Polyphemus.

The story also feels very real and easy to connect with today. We see Penelope staying strong and faithful at home while tricking the greedy suitors, Telemachus growing up while searching for his lost father, and Odysseus losing his ships and crew but refusing to give up. It is a family story about loyalty that anyone can understand.

02

Literary Elements

Significant Themes, Characters & Lessons

Several important themes and characters make The Odyssey truly unforgettable:

  • Homecoming (Nostos): Going home is not just about traveling back to an island; it is about rebuilding your life, standing up for your family, and keeping your identity.
  • Cleverness vs. Foolish Pride: Odysseus wins when he uses patience and plans ahead. But when he lets his ego take over (like bragging about his real name to the Cyclops), he causes trouble for himself and his men, showing that too much pride is dangerous.
  • Penelope’s Equal Wit: Penelope is just as smart and patient as Odysseus. Her test with their secret olive-tree bed proves that their bond is built on mutual trust and cleverness.
  • Hospitality and Respect (Xenia): Welcoming guests with kindness was a basic rule of ancient life, showing the clear difference between decent people and cruel, lawless bullies.
03

Personal Connection

Personal Connections & Realizations

Reading The Odyssey taught me that perseverance and family are the ultimate anchors of human life. No matter how many challenges, detours, and unexpected setbacks we face, staying determined and holding onto the people who love and support us gives us the courage to keep moving forward.

As an Information Technology student getting ready to graduate and enter the working world, Odysseus's long journey feels a lot like the real-life effort of finishing college and building a career. There are times when codes fail, projects break down, and you feel exhausted. Just like Odysseus holding onto a raft in the middle of a storm, success is not just about luck; it comes from staying patient, solving problems step by step, and remembering why we work hard: to make our families proud and build a good future.

A Final Thought
"No matter how many challenges and detours we face, perseverance, clear purpose, and the love of family are what guide us safely home."
- Personal Realization on Homer’s The Odyssey
