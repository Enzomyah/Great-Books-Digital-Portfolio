Section 05 - Personal Reflection

Reflections on
My Literary Journey.

Five Questions - A Reader's Growth

These five reflections trace my personal journey through the Great Books course - examining how my understanding changed, what challenged me, which ideas left the deepest impression, and how I will carry the lessons of great literature into my future.

01

Reflection One

My Changing Understanding of Literature

How has my understanding and appreciation of literature changed throughout the course?

Before taking this Great Books class, I honestly thought of literature mostly as school requirements: reading long books, summarizing chapters, and memorizing character names for quizzes. Over the semester, my view changed completely. I realized that great books are actually real, emotional records of human struggles, cultural values, and life questions passed down over hundreds of years.

Works like The Odyssey and The Ramayana showed me that ancient epics were not just fun adventure stories; they were practical guides that taught ancient communities about loyalty, honor, and duty. Reading literature now feels like having a real conversation with history, pushing me to look past simple plots and find the deeper life lessons that connect us all.

02

Reflection Two

Challenges & Growth

What challenges did I encounter when reading and analyzing literary masterpieces, and how did I overcome them?

Reading these ancient and medieval works was definitely challenging at first. The formal poetic language of Homer, the huge family trees and complicated names in The Mahabharata, the quiet palace customs of ancient Japan in The Tale of Genji, and the deep poetic speeches in The Book of Job felt very far away from my everyday life as an IT student.

I handled these difficulties by slowing down my reading, learning the basic history behind each story, and using the literary lenses we learned in class. Taking the time to understand why an author wrote a certain way turned my confusion into genuine appreciation. It taught me that being patient and keeping an open mind are essential whenever I face complicated or unfamiliar material.

03

Reflection Three

The Literary Lens That Influenced Me Most

Which literary lens or approach influenced my interpretation of literature the most, and why?

The Historical / Contextual Lens and the Ecocritical Lens influenced me the most. The Historical Lens showed me how much real-life events shape storytelling, like seeing how The Song of Roland changed a real mountain skirmish into anti-Saracen Crusade propaganda.

At the same time, the Ecocritical Lens changed how I connect with literature and nature, especially when reading The Book of Job. It decentered human self-absorption and revealed the awe-inspiring majesty and autonomy of the non-human cosmos in the whirlwind speech, teaching deep humility before creation.

04

Reflection Four

Realizations About Life & Human Nature

What important realizations about life, society, culture, or human nature have I gained?

Across all seven masterpieces, my biggest realization is that human nature does not change. The stubborn pride that brought down Roland and Ravana, the family jealousy that tore apart the cousins in The Mahabharata, the quiet sadness of women trapped by unfair rules in The Tale of Genji, and the confusion of innocent suffering in Job are the exact same struggles people deal with today, just in ancient settings.

These stories showed me that arrogance, jealousy, and quick temper always end up hurting people. On the other hand, virtues like patience, loyalty, humility, and genuine empathy are what keep families and communities together. Even with thousands of years between us, human beings share the exact same heart.

05

Reflection Five

Applying What I Have Learned

How can I apply the lessons and values learned from Great Books in my personal, academic, or future professional life?

As a graduating Information Technology student planning to work in software and tech systems, the lessons from Great Books are surprisingly practical. Technology is never just about code; it is designed for and used by real people with real feelings and needs. Learning how to analyze complex stories helps me look at problems from multiple angles, think through the long-term impact of decisions, and build technology with a strong sense of ethics and care.

In my personal life, Odysseus's perseverance, Rama's focus on doing what is right, and the lesson from The Panchatantra about not jumping to conclusions will guide me every day. Taking time to listen, checking facts before judging, and taking care of my family and loved ones are values that will stay with me long after graduation.

In Closing

A Reader, A Thinker, A Student of Life

The Great Books course has left a lasting impression on how I read, think, and see the world around me. Reading literature is not a passive chore; it asks us to care, to understand others, and to let ourselves grow from the stories we experience.

"Studying Great Books showed me that exploring classic literature is really about exploring ourselves: our shared struggles, our hopes, and our constant search for meaning, love, and truth."
