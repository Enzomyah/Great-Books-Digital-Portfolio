Section 04 - Summary of Learning

What I Learned
Through Literature.

Four Areas of Growth - Great Books Course

The Great Books course offered more than a reading list - it provided a framework for understanding literature, humanity, and myself as a critical thinker. The following sections summarize what I learned across four key areas throughout the course.

01

Learning Area One

Understanding Literary Masterpieces

Taking this course helped me truly understand and appreciate classic stories from different cultures and time periods. I learned that great literature does not just appear out of nowhere; it comes from real historical moments, cultural beliefs, and social struggles that shaped what the authors wrote about.

Reading works from ancient Greece (The Odyssey), ancient India (The Ramayana, The Mahabharata, and The Panchatantra), medieval France (The Song of Roland), ancient Japan (The Tale of Genji), and biblical literature (The Book of Job) showed me that these books are not dusty museum pieces. Instead, they are living records of how people across history struggled with the exact same questions we face today: how to protect our families, what it means to be a loyal friend, how to handle grief, and how to stand up for what is right.

02

Learning Area Two

Applying Literary Lenses

One of the most practical skills I learned in Great Books is how to use different literary lenses. A literary lens is like putting on a pair of glasses that highlights specific details in a story, whether that is history, psychology, gender roles, or story structure.

Using the Archetypal Lens helped me see Odysseus's voyage in The Odyssey as a universal journey of growing up and finding your way home. The Feminist Lens brought attention to Sita's moral resilience, agency, and trials within patriarchal expectations in The Ramayana. The Psychoanalytic Lens helped me understand the deep jealousy, pride, and psychological turmoil driving the feud in The Mahabharata. The Formalist / New Critical Lens highlighted the clever frame-tale structure and animal dialogues of The Panchatantra. The Historical / Contextual Lens exposed how The Song of Roland turned a real-life skirmish into Crusade propaganda. The Biographical Lens illuminated how Murasaki Shikibu's own life as an observant Heian court lady shaped the nuanced world of The Tale of Genji, while the Ecocritical Lens revealed the grandeur of the non-human cosmos and ecological humility in The Book of Job's whirlwind speech. Learning these lenses showed me that a great book has many different layers of meaning depending on how you look at it.

03

Learning Area Three

Literature & Human Experience

Great books survive through the centuries because they talk about basic human experiences that everyone goes through: love, heartbreak, fairness, belonging, and the fear of losing what we care about. Reading these stories showed me how literature works like a mirror, showing both the best and worst parts of human nature.

Whether reading about Rama giving up his throne for family honor, Job crying in the ashes after losing his children, or Prince Genji realizing that youth and beauty don't last forever (mono no aware), I realized that people thousands of years ago felt the exact same pain and joy that we feel today. Even as an IT student living in a high-tech world, these ancient stories reminded me that while technology changes fast, the human heart and our need for connection stay the same.

04

Learning Area Four

Critical Thinking & Interpretation

The biggest takeaway from this course for my everyday life is how it trained my critical thinking. Reading closely taught me not to just accept things at face value, but to ask questions, consider different viewpoints, and find evidence before making a judgment.

Before this subject, I used to read stories just to find out what happens next. Great Books taught me to look deeper: to notice hidden motivations, see how historical bias shapes the narrative, and understand why characters react the way they do. In my major, Information Technology, we solve problems, design user-friendly systems, and write clean code. Being able to break down complex stories, think logically, and understand people's feelings with empathy is a skill that will help me greatly in my career.

In Summary

What Literature Taught Me

The Great Books course challenged me to think more carefully, be more empathetic, and see the world through other people's eyes. Reading works from different countries and centuries taught me that literature is not just about memorizing old plots; it is an ongoing conversation about what it means to be human.

"Reading these seven masterpieces showed me that a true college education is not just about memorizing technical facts, but about building empathy, clear thinking, and a genuine care for people and life."
